### A — Research & Source Qualification

A operates on **one bounded research topic at a time**. Examples of separate future research domains include macro-regime direction, lower-timeframe execution, sentiment analysis, price action, and risk profiling/sizing. A research commission should be broad enough to discover useful approaches but not become a search for the entire trading system.

A receives three Public inputs: a research brief, a **Level 1 trusted-source register**, and a known excluded/unacceptable-source register. Level 1 is preferred but **not an exclusive whitelist**. A may search outside it where useful.

A may return **no more than 30 candidate sources per research commission**. This is an operating constraint, not presently part of W5.1/W6.1.

A may judge **source quality and relevance**, but not whether a trading system/strategy is good. Every source touched remains in the complete Research Evidence Register, including excluded sources. Nothing is silently deleted.

A should record whether each source is Level 1 or outside Level 1 and produce an executive overview such as sources reviewed, recommended Include/Exclude, Level 1/outside mix and evidence gaps. This is intended to help you see whether the research area appears well documented.

A's recommendation is **not admission authority**. The control sequence is:

> **A recommends → source checker verifies → Human Operator approves.**

The checker verifies at least source existence/identity and claimed title/author. Human then performs a quick legitimacy/relevance review.

Individual bad sources are held/rejected; **one bad source does not stop the entire batch**.

If the surviving evidence is too thin, you decide whether to **recommission A with different descriptors/search framing**. A thin evidence base is not automatically padded with weaker sources.

The complete A register remains the audit trail. The A→B handoff is deliberately different:

> **Operator-approved source list + corresponding source documents personally downloaded by the Human Operator.**

Excluded/rejected sources do not cross. **B is not permitted to retrieve replacement/source documents itself.**

## B — Evidence Synthesis & Strategy Formulation

B works on **one commissioned research domain at a time**. If the commission is Macro Regime Direction, its candidates remain Macro Regime Direction approaches.

If B notices something useful belonging to another domain—e.g. lower-timeframe execution—it records it as a **new research lead**. It cannot bypass A by using its own knowledge to expand scope. That new topic requires its own A research commission.

B is the major **reasoning/design stage**:

> **A judges sources → B judges ideas → C tests implementations.**

B may synthesise multiple sources, reconcile disagreements and create **novel combinations/hypotheses not explicitly proposed by any single source**. However, it must distinguish:

> **Evidence-backed statement → source ID(s)**  
> **B-created inference/assumption → explicitly labelled as such.**

A grounding checker checks B's claims against the approved A evidence package and alerts you to unsupported/misattributed reasoning. The checker does not make the release decision.

B has **no independent research authority**. If evidence is missing, B reports the gap; it does not search externally itself.

B ultimately ranks candidate strategy specifications and seeks to produce **three feasible candidates for C**. They can be materially different approaches within the same research domain.

A Candidate Strategy Specification should eventually contain enough for C to build it, including the proposed strategy/model logic, mathematical/statistical model, evidence basis, B-created assumptions/inferences, required data and likely source/access conditions, dependencies/implementation requirements, research-derived starting values, and **B-recommended parameter/search ranges**.

B owns the **search envelope**. It should reason about ranges supported by the research, with reasonable flexibility, rather than giving C unlimited parameter space.

The Human Operator's B→C review is a **feasibility/permissibility review**, not an attempt to predict whether the strategy will work. Important checks include data availability/access permission, prohibited or black-box dependencies, boundary violations, grounding issues and whether the specification is sufficiently buildable.

If it passes those checks, **C gets to empirically settle whether B's recommendation was good**. You do not substitute your preference for B's ranking merely because you're sceptical.

Human decisions are:

> **RELEASE / RETURN TO B / REJECT**

A returned candidate is reformulated and then **B reassesses/reranks it**; it does not automatically retain its previous ranking.

If a candidate fails feasibility, B can present the next-ranked candidate. Continue until three candidates survive or the candidate pool is exhausted. **Exhaustion is an acceptable finding**; we do not weaken the gate merely to manufacture three candidates.

The complete B candidate/ranking/rejection/version record remains behind. C receives only Human-released candidates.

We also decided to create a small **B→C Operator Decision Record** containing candidate ID/version, B rank, data feasibility, boundary/dependency findings, RELEASE/RETURN/REJECT, reason and date/version.

## C — Local Integration & Stage 1 Backtesting

C is a **development/search environment**. It is deliberately allowed to learn from Stage 1 results.

The authority split we settled on is important:

> **B controls the strategy concept and search space.**  
> **Human Operator controls the evidence and yardstick.**  
> **C controls the adaptive search path.**

B fixes strategy logic, mathematical model, permitted parameter ranges and the maximum number of parameter variants **before C sees Stage 1 results**.

The maximum variant count may differ between B candidates depending on search-space complexity. We have **not chosen actual numbers yet**.

C may adaptively choose the next parameter combination based on earlier Stage 1 results. That's intentional optimisation. But C cannot expand B's parameter ranges or silently change the strategy hypothesis after seeing results.

We distinguished three kinds of change:

**Parameter optimisation:** same strategy, different permitted parameter values. Counts toward the optimisation-variant limit.

**Implementation correction/refactoring:** changes code while preserving the same strategy behaviour/specification. Does **not** consume a parameter variant merely because code changed.

**Strategy-logic change:** new variable, indicator, condition, mathematical relationship, expanded search range, etc. C cannot make this autonomously. It records an empirical improvement hypothesis and escalates through the Human Operator to B for a new specification/version.

### Stage 1 evidence boundary

You prepare and freeze **one Stage 1 OHLC dataset** used across strategies and parameter variants. C has no MT5 access.

The same dataset across variants gives a common development ruler and prevents C from selecting its own favourable evidence.

We discussed a future **common market-data API/interface**. The same interface could be used by C and D while environment-specific access controls determine the underlying source:

> **C binding → frozen Stage 1 OHLC export**  
> **D binding → MT5 + authorised unseen OOS window**

The important point is identical interface, different evidence permissions. C should be technically unable to query D's OOS evidence.

### Cloud AI inside C

Cloud AI is not completely prohibited. It may generate **reusable modules from Public/sanitised specifications**.

However, meaningful C integration involves your proprietary lower-timeframe execution/risk/other strategy components. Once proprietary components, combined architecture, parameters or actual Stage 1 results are involved, the work is **Restricted/local**.

Removing parameter values alone does **not** make proprietary architecture Public.

### Stage 1 harness

A major W6 design change is that the mandatory **9-pip friction should be Operator-owned evaluation configuration**, not something candidate code controls.

The intended architecture is:

> candidate produces trades → Operator-controlled harness applies **9 pips total per completed round-trip trade** → deterministic metrics → frozen Stage 1 gates.

We have **not built this harness** and have deliberately parked its exact arithmetic/implementation for later.

This changes the W5.3/W5.4 rogue-friction proof. We do **not erase that history**. The W5 challenge exposed the weakness; W6 improved the architecture. The future test should establish whether candidate-controlled code/configuration can bypass the Operator-owned friction rule.

### Stage 1 hard gates

You freeze all PASS/FAIL gates before optimisation. C cannot relax them after seeing results.

Martingale, grid and averaging-down remain **absolute disqualifiers**, regardless of performance.

Any new dependency introduced by C must be declared and checked. An unapproved/black-box dependency causes **HOLD/FAIL + Human alert**. C cannot approve a dependency simply because it improves performance.

C's optimisation objective will be **multi-objective**, not CPC-only. We have **not yet defined the exact multi-objective formula/weights**. Hard gates remain non-tradeable constraints.

### Complete optimisation record

Every tested parameter variant—PASS and FAIL—is recorded.

Because C is adaptive, we specifically want:

> Variant ID → parameter values → why selected → result → gate outcomes → PASS/FAIL → what C inferred → why the next variant was selected.

We do not retain only winners.

However, **only PASS variants proceed to D**. Failed variants remain in C's audit record and are dead for promotion.

We originally considered grouping passing variants. We explicitly rejected that. **Every Stage 1 PASS variant goes to D.**

C does not have to identify one winner. Multi-objective results may have legitimate trade-offs; D will independently challenge all PASS variants.

If the precommitted optimisation limit is exhausted with **zero PASS**, C FAILs that B candidate and alerts you. You decide:

> **CLOSE / RETURN TO B / RESEARCH GAP → A**

C cannot award itself more variants or widen its search space after seeing failure.

## D — Stage 2 OOS Validation

D is deliberately **non-creative**.

> **B designs → C searches → D falsifies → E decides.**

D must not recommend strategy improvements, new indicators, parameters or redesigns.

### Common data interface

We decided C and D can use the same market-data interface while their environments control the source:

> **C → frozen exported Stage 1 OHLC**  
> **D → MT5 over the Human Operator-controlled unseen period**

D may query through that interface but cannot choose/change the OOS evidence boundary.

### Validation cohort

All C PASS variants frozen **before D results are opened** form a validation cohort.

Every member gets **the same fixed OOS date/sample**—your “single ruler” principle.

We decided against multiple walk-forward windows for this design. Each frozen candidate receives **one valid OOS validation against that common unseen dataset**.

Once results are reviewed and can influence redesign, that OOS window is **consumed** for future redesigned candidates.

### Same endpoint, different evidence

D uses the **same endpoint gates** rather than inventing a harder standard. The distinction between C and D is the evidence, not the hurdle.

C PASS means:

> **eligible to be challenged.**

D PASS means:

> **survived the endpoint requirements on unseen evidence.**

D includes the ≥100 OOS-trade requirement already carried from Gate 1/W5. W5v1.4_records

### D outcomes

We deliberately established three outcomes:

**PASS** — valid OOS test and every endpoint gate passes → eligible for E.

**FAIL** — valid OOS test but one or more endpoint gates fail → candidate/version stops; Human alerted with full diagnostic package.

**INVALID/HOLD** — validation itself is untrustworthy: wrong/incomplete data, fingerprint mismatch, execution failure, harness problem, etc. → no conclusion about the strategy.

An INVALID/HOLD attempt remains permanently recorded. Once the validation problem is corrected, D reruns the **exact same frozen candidate against the same intended OOS window**. No code/parameter changes are allowed between the invalid attempt and rerun.

The exceptional outlier trigger also applies in D:

> **CPC >2.0 or Sharpe >2.0 → HOLD for Human integrity audit before E.**

### D records everything

For every candidate, D retains the full validation record: candidate/version/fingerprint, OOS evidence identity/window, execution configuration, complete trade ledger, 9-pip-adjusted results, CPC, drawdown, activity count, trade count, every gate outcome, PASS/FAIL/INVALID-HOLD and diagnostic breakdowns.

You specifically want the **full diagnostic package on D FAIL**, not merely the failed metric.

But once you inspect and use those OOS diagnostics to redesign the strategy, that evidence has become development knowledge.

After D FAIL, you decide:

> **CLOSE / RETURN TO B / RESEARCH GAP → A**

A redesigned candidate informed by those results eventually needs **fresh unseen OOS evidence**.

### D→E information boundary

Every D PASS goes to E. D does **not rank survivors**.

E receives the complete D validation evidence needed for commissioning.

E does **not need C's optimisation history**. Specifically, C's failed variants, adaptive search path, relative Stage 1 rankings and optimiser reasoning stay behind.

The raw OOS market dataset can remain in the controlled data layer; E receives its identity/window and resulting validation evidence rather than needing a duplicated raw dataset.

# W6.2 — Operating interpretation

## What this handoff is deciding

B→C is **not** deciding whether the strategy is good or whether it will make money.

It is deciding:

> **Is this a valid experiment that C is allowed and able to run?**

A strange or apparently weak strategy may still proceed if it is grounded, clear, feasible, permitted and testable. Its performance merit is deliberately left to C and independent OOS validation in D.

## What crosses B → C

Only the **Human-approved Candidate Strategy Specification** crosses.

The specification tells C:

- what strategy/module to build;
- what the strategy is trying to do;
- the mathematical/model logic;
- what input data it requires;
- what output it should produce;
- which approved evidence supports the design;
- which parts are B-created assumptions/inferences;
- what parameter ranges C may optimise;
- what dependencies it requires.

C does **not** receive:

- A's underlying research reports;
- excluded/rejected research sources;
- B's rejected candidates;
- B's alternative candidate ideas;
- B's complete ranking history.

B does the research synthesis and strategy reasoning. C builds and tests the released specification.

---

## HOLD does not mean REJECT

A HOLD means the candidate **cannot enter C yet**.

Examples:

- required dataset not yet available;
- legitimate dependency needs Human approval/installation;
- specification contains ambiguity;
- C currently lacks the required capability;
- grounding checker has an unresolved finding;
- Human release has not been recorded.

The Human Operator decides what happens next.

A prohibited/black-box dependency or another unacceptable boundary issue may result in **RETURN TO B or REJECT**, rather than simply waiting for a resource to become available.
